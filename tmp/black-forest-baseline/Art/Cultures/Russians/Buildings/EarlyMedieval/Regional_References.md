# Early Medieval regional references

The user requested a clearer Slavic/Rus identity on October 2, 2026. The visual
direction uses ninth-to-eleventh-century Ladoga and Novgorod as early Rus
regional anchors. This is a coherent game art direction, not a reconstruction
of one excavated compound or a claim that the region had a single ethnicity.

## Research and visual use

- [Old Ladoga excavation report, Institute of Archaeology RAS](https://archaeolog.ru/about/history/expeditions-1990-present/raskopki-v-staroy-ladoge-staroladozhskaya-ekspeditsiya-iimk-ran):
  describes Ladoga as a port and trading settlement, illustrates buildings
  from the middle of the ninth century and discusses the eighth-to-tenth-century
  archaeological sequence. Use timber settlement construction, river cargo,
  iron weapons and a varied material palette. The report also contains later
  fortresses and twelfth-to-thirteenth-century jewelry; those are not used as
  ninth-century building evidence.
- [Suzdal fortification research announcement, Institute of Archaeology RAS](https://archaeolog.ru/press/pubs/avsz-13-ukrepleniya-suzdalya-i-oruzhie-iz-plyosa):
  summarizes a proposed sequence of early ditches and earthworks before the
  first half of the eleventh century, followed by a Rus timber-and-earth wall
  interpreted as separate log cells. Use broad timber-framed boundary tops
  and cross-ties as an age-progression cue. This is a regional analogy and a
  research interpretation, not a securely attested universal ninth-century
  enclosure plan. The announcement's later medieval weapon collection is
  excluded from this age's reference evidence.
- [Medieval Novgorod exhibition, Novgorod Museum](https://novgorodmuseum.ru/visit/sobytiya/legkoj-postupyu-po-mostovoj.-obuv-drevnego-novgoroda-x-xv-vv.):
  describes the city as a trade crossroads and its wooden streets. Use wooden
  boardwalks in the town and working yards. The exhibition covers the tenth
  through fifteenth centuries; its specifically twelfth-to-thirteenth-century
  shoemaking workshops are not treated as early Rus evidence.
- [Log buildings of ancient Novgorod, Russian Archaeology 2007, issue 1](https://archaeolog.ru/media/books_sov_archaeology/RA_2007_1.pdf):
  the indexed paper describes buildings from the second half of the tenth
  century onward. This supports the log-building direction. Only the indexed
  extract was consulted; no exact roof or house plan is attributed to this
  source.

## Interpretation and chronological limits

Weathered grey split-plank roof surfaces, roof layouts, notched beam details,
the combined town block and specialized recruitment yards are visual design
choices. Upper building forms are less securely preserved than ground plans.
The crossed timber caps simplify fortifications for a 90-degree overhead icon;
they do not depict the full engineering of a wall.

The traction stone thrower, mobile covered siege tower and ram preserve the
game's siege-production role and age progression. No specific early Rus machine
plan was established by this research. Chimney count, kiln placement, exact
river-boat form, equipment arrangements and target/dummy layouts similarly
serve gameplay clarity rather than an excavated blueprint.

The [Novgorod estate reconstruction study](https://archaeolog.ru/media/periodicals/agis/AGIS-11/Petrov/petrov.html)
was checked for methodological context. Its modeled estate is fourteenth-century
and its upper building forms are hypothetical, so it is not used as literal
ninth-to-eleventh-century architecture. Later museum timber houses, ornate
Muscovite carving and onion-domed silhouettes are also excluded.
