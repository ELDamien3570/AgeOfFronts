Navigation implementation: **2b44a7356ce3bf6a96d69649c893976d817178e7**. Final invalid-landing validation: **1ebaec057304b79fddb4f52f1a118aa5e02d8ef8**. Both are published; the final Oracle release is verified below.

## Findings and correction

Rejected online commands replaced the brand subtitle with their rejection text, expanding the header and shifting the battlefield. The subtitle now remains "Online match · server hosted"; normal rejections use the existing notification.

Troop move commands rejected water before reaching researched shore transport. The shared transport domain now admits water destinations, physically boards and sails its cargo, and retains it afloat. Those temporary vessels can subsequently sail, stop and unload. Cargo capacity, research, normal movement commands and landing clearance remain authoritative. Full and sparse network packets now include the presentation-only shore-transfer phase and destination; the encoder previously omitted them, which the successful-water multiplayer acceptance test exposed.

Repeated AI orders cancelled unfinished shore admission while squads still had hold orders. Repeated unchanged transfer requests now share the existing admission, and the periodic AI controller leaves pending transfers alone. Cross-faction war-policy revisions also invalidated civilian trade and unrelated military planning. Route fences now describe the relevant faction's actual entry permissions; civilian trade uses the existing wall/alliance rules, independently of military declarations.

Whole-coast collection and sorting still delayed crossings even after cancellation was fixed. An immutable spatial coast index now yields exact nearest-first candidates with a checkpointed frontier. Each query expansion performs at most four heap insertions. Its ordering matches the original complete sort, including ties, and blocked candidates continue through the frontier rather than truncating coast coverage.

Water planning admitted navigable passages, but ship movement subsequently applied land-entry war policy to claimed coastal water, stopped boats midway, and interpreted the cleared destination as arrival. Navigable water no longer uses the land-entry restriction. Attacks and physical landings retain their live checks. Automatic ferries complete their committed passage and return real cargo to their departure when landing permission is withdrawn; a checkpointed historical premature landing can resume its remaining physical path. Manual redirection explicitly changes a temporary vessel to afloat control.

Some move orders repeatedly repaired paths to occupied destinations. Standalone squads now re-admit a nearby available formation slot while preserving queued player orders; army-owned movement remains with its army. A stationary move order is presented as "Waiting for clearance", rather than asserting movement. This is not a guarantee that every crowded or politically forbidden destination is reachable.

Staged AI warships held movement leases, suppressing the ordinary patrol controller, while their fleet merely held a port anchor. The fleet owner now issues bounded local defensive patrols through normal ship admission. The paid transport planner also stops purchasing a vessel when it lacks eligible cargo, a legal landing or an escort; the retained checkpoint's three unused paid transports came from unsupported missions, rather than a broken player warship controller.

Rendered acceptance exposed one further gap: deferred unloading admitted invalid water/distant/moving-vessel landings, then failed silently during footprint planning. The domain now rejects those impossible requests before admission and preserves the vessel, cargo and existing planning state. Valid physical landings retain deferred footprint admission and commit-time checks.
