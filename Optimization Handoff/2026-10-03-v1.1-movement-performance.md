# V1.1 movement, transport and player response

Working branch: `V1.1-`. Stable comparison: `d5ba9c405866d073c1f2f448520313c8d8692d90`.

## Implemented

- Friendly and allied squads may overlap in movement, formation placement, recruitment clearance and transport landing. Terrain, fortifications and full enemy swept collision remain authoritative. Friendly passage reservations and reciprocal crowd-yield leases no longer delay movement. Legacy checkpoint fields remain readable.
- Large human land selections use a promptly committed lead route, followed by cohorts of at most ten squads sharing its long corridor. AI cohorts retain their larger size. A fixed 128-operation foreground preparation allowance supplements the normal background budget. Selection size is unchanged.
- Automatic crossings launch capacity-sized boats progressively. Passengers may share a boarding point, and friendly landing positions may overlap on tiny islands. Committed boats retain physical ownership of their passengers when the remainder is cancelled or restored.
- Human shore work receives a fixed, additional 256-operation allowance. Coast search, staging, manual boarding and landing filter that allowance to human work; ordinary background work retains its allowance. Pending starts rotate instead of repeatedly serving the same first entries.
- Both exact routing and the earlier navigation/AI command queue prioritize human work with bounded background progress. The earlier queue uses the authoritative tick as its scheduling phase, preserving checkpoint determinism without a clock-dependent policy.
- Nearby idle AI expansion squads share one frontier decision and march command in groups of up to sixteen, partitioned by land component and local area. Combat, replenishment and asset reservations remain individual. This does not grant Army technology or combat bonuses.
- AI footprint permissions share a derived cache for one tick. Ownership writes and operation, threat and diplomacy revisions invalidate it immediately. Standalone routing drains also retain a scoped cache. No result survives a tick or an external command boundary.
- Player attack intent searches for a replacement when its target disappears, embarks, becomes non-hostile, becomes unreachable or leaves its bounded pursuit area. A non-fighting stalled attacker may select an alternative. Searches cover twelve tiles around the squad and sixteen around the attack anchor, with at most 32 scans per tick and a ten-tick retry interval. Failed target/tile pairs are suppressed for sixty ticks. A missing-target search expires after sixty ticks. Explicit Hold and queued orders retain precedence. Pursuit state is checkpointed.
- Still-valid cohort and coast intents re-quote superseded routes after terrain-cost changes. Ownership, order, generation and permission changes still cancel them. This avoids abandoning remaining passengers halfway through a large crossing when a forest/road cost revision changes.
- Oracle match capacity is one, with user approval. The live stable image was recreated with this configuration only, after idle checks and database/configuration backups. Local browser AI skirmishes are unaffected. V1.1 source has not been deployed.

## Measurement protocol

Late-game comparisons restore the same 40-minute, 500-size Valles Kairulia checkpoint with ten AI, 25 tribes and all five AI flags enabled, then replay to minute 42. Each replay records phases and an inspector CPU profile. Timing windows contain the last 256 samples, rather than the entire replay. Changes to movement and AI behavior cause the worlds to diverge; population counts and shipping activity must accompany timing comparisons.

Oracle comparisons run in separate network-isolated containers using the deployed Node 24.18.0 image and mounted candidate source. Limits are two CPUs, 2 GiB and 128 processes. Every run checks `activeMatches: 0` and stops if a live match appears. The live application is not replaced by benchmark source.

First motion means displacement greater than eight fixed coordinate units. First motion, every squad being admitted, first boat creation and final arrival are separate outcomes. The measured benchmark wall times exclude browser rendering, network round trips and real-time tick pacing. Four synthetic orders in one saved world do not establish a population p95.

## Player latency and correctness

| Scenario | Stable | Candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 squads, open land, first motion | 44 ticks / 2.2 game seconds | 4 ticks / 0.2 game seconds |
| 40 squads, held friendly one-tile gap | No completed passage in 2,400 ticks | All 40 pass |
| Held enemy one-tile gap | Enemy blocks passage | Enemy blocks passage; no breach |
| 100 squads, river crossing | Formation rejected | Ten boats; all 100 arrive |
| 50 squads, four-tile island | Formation rejected | Five boats; all 50 arrive |
| Four busy-world 100-squad land orders, Oracle ARM | Two do not start within 240 ticks; other two start at 110 and 128 ticks | Every order starts at four ticks; 193–332 ms benchmark wall time |
| Four busy-world 100-squad crossings, Oracle ARM | Intermediate candidate initially needed 42–119 ticks before its dedicated foreground allowance | Final candidate starts at 7–10 ticks; 269–702 ms benchmark wall time |
| All passengers admitted in final busy-world crossing trials | — | 100/100 in every trial, all receipts executed; 168–228 ticks / 8.4–11.4 game seconds |
| 100 player attackers lose their target | No replacement attacks | All 100 retarget within four ticks; explicit Hold causes zero scans |

Normal transit and landing take longer than admission. Progressive land and transport orders can have an already moving lead/boat while the parent command remains deferred. Cancelling or losing remaining squads does not undo physically committed movement. Enemies may legitimately prevent a landing or kill passengers.

## CPU and capacity

The stable ARM replay takes 105.37 seconds; the final frozen-source candidate replay takes 89.65 seconds, about 14.9% less. Its final-window mean tick is 33.94 ms versus 39.90 ms, and p95 is 49.33 ms versus 53.61 ms. The candidate world contains 505 squads, 24 ships, 666 buildings and 54 traders; the stable world contains 515, 12, 661 and 50 respectively.

The final local clean replay takes 69.69 seconds versus 73.95 seconds. Its final-window p95 is **42.66 ms versus 37.28 ms**, despite a slightly lower mean. The improvement is not universal: additional successful naval activity and route retries change the work distribution. Do not claim that every tail metric improved.

Two simultaneous candidate late-game simulations on Oracle's two-core ARM host produce tick p95s of 75.83 and 93.69 ms, and maxima of 300.13 and 233.44 ms, against the 50 ms tick budget. This is a compute-capacity failure, not evidence of network congestion. One active match is the current operational limit; supporting multiple late-game matches needs more CPU or further measured reduction.

Initial passive live telemetry also showed mean ticks around 46.84 ms, p95 63.28 ms and routing p95 27.10 ms, with no socket backlog. Repeated radius/permission reads, avoidance and route construction were substantial CPU consumers. Worker encoding elapsed time included queueing and must not be interpreted as pure compression CPU.

GC trace totals include startup. The stable local process has 1,216 events and 1,911.9 ms cumulative pauses, with a 10.3 ms maximum; the final process has 960 events and 2,102.2 ms cumulative pauses, with a 14.8 ms maximum. These are additive pauses, not the sole explanation for the much larger capacity spikes. [Node's GC diagnostics guide](https://github.com/nodejs/learn/blob/main/pages/diagnostics/memory/using-gc-traces.md) describes the tracing method.

## Changes measured and rejected

A bounded capture-circle coordinate cache preserved world results but did not demonstrate a convincing whole-replay improvement. It was removed. An intermediate queue implementation allocated/scanned arrays unnecessarily during all-AI work; the retained implementation keeps ordinary FIFO behavior when no human task is pending.

Coverage includes friendly overlap, enemy blocking, full tiny-island unload, progressive transport restore/cancellation, staging cost-revision retries, ordered Shift behavior, player target loss, unreachable-target suppression, disconnected targets, bounded pursuit, explicit Hold, checkpoint equivalence and queue fairness.

## Remaining qualification

These measurements demonstrate server/simulation response improvements, not ten-rendered-browser or public Internet latency certification. Single-match ARM p95 is close to budget, occasional ticks still exceed it, and enemy-heavy bottlenecks remain more expensive than free movement. The isolated enemy-gap fixture increases total CPU as more friendly squads reach the defended gap simultaneously; removing friendly collision is not a universal CPU win.

Keep profiling capture/fortification checks, cold hierarchy construction and repeated failed trade routes before raising capacity. Collect command-to-first-visible-motion telemetry in real Chrome and Opera multiplayer playtests. Retest additional seeds, later ages, larger armies and sustained combat before claiming a universal subsecond pathing guarantee.
