## How it works today

### Who runs what

- Production matches always run with `DEFAULT_AI_POLICIES` (`content/AiPolicies.ts` 3–9: `aiEconomy`, `deferredPlanning`, `aiDefenses`, `aiNaval`, `aiWarPolicy` all true) and `diplomacyRules: "raids-v1"` (`LiveMatch.ts:147`). `Skirmish.developAi` (Simulation.ts 5253) returns immediately when an `Expansion` exists, and the non-economy fallback in `Expansion.thinkProgression` (1331–1500) only runs when `economy.enabled(player)` is false, so **for nations the `AiEconomicDirector` is the only economic brain**. `Skirmish.thinkNavy` (5374) likewise runs only when `economy.naval.enabled(player)` is false.
- `AiEconomicDirector.enabled` (99–106) requires `player.kind === "regular"`, so **tribes never get the director**. Tribes use `Expansion.thinkTribeDevelopment` (1521–1570): advance when `tribeAdvanceRejection` and `advanceRejection` allow; research the *first catalogue technology of the current age that is not rejected* (no utility, 1545–1547); one coastal port; then walk `TRIBE_BUILDING_ORDER` with `tribeBuildingLimit` (1 of every type, 2 of military types, unlimited mines, `TribeDevelopment.ts` 40–43), at most one build per 3 s. Tribes *do* get fleet missions: `AiNavalPlanner.enabled` (186–193) admits tribes whenever `aiEconomy && aiNaval && deferredPlanning`, with `TRIBE_WARSHIP_CAP = 24` (`FactionRules.ts` 31–36).
- Promotion (`Simulation.promoteTribes`, 5412–5420) flips `player.kind` to `"regular"` in place, after which the director takes over without a generation bump; `tribePlans` are garbage-collected in `Expansion.beforeStep` (1088).
- Automatic production (`Supply` → `automaticProduction`, Supply.ts 429–445) is shared by humans and AI; the director only contributes `aiDemand` (equipment/material targets) and `protectedInputs` (ledger-reserved items) through `AiEconomicDirector.production` (241–258), which `Expansion` wires at 256.

### Director cadence and budgets (`AiEconomicDirector.step`, 196–240)

Every tick, in order: `expireOwnership` (ledger + lease expiry, generation checks, existence checks), `recovery.step`, then a fixed **global** background allowance shared by all factions: boundaries ≤32, fronts ≤16, `navalFacts.step(48 − frontWork)`, `naval.step(16)`, army ≤24, trade quotes ≤8, coasts ≤8, cities get the remainder (≥16), then defenses and modern fronts (32). Then, **only on every second tick** (`world.tick % secondsToTicks(.15)` = `% 2`, line 227), the round-robin cursor finds the first enabled nation whose `nextDecision` has passed, sets `nextDecision = tick + 3 s` and calls `decide` for **that one player** (`break`, line 238). So the whole match gets at most 4 decisions/s, and each nation gets one decision every ≥3 s; with more than 12 enabled nations the cadence degrades linearly (44 promoted factions → one decision per ~11 s each).

### One decision (`decide`, 259–478)

1. Build an `AiEconomicSnapshot` (`AiEconomicSnapshot.ts` 35–113): copies of the owner's buildings/squads/ships/jobs, liquid gold/reserves/items, `incoming` (outputs of running production jobs), `reserveIncome`, a hard-coded `goldIncome = 20 + floor(land/40)` that mirrors `produceReserves` (Simulation.ts 3684–3688, trade income is not modelled), headroom vs squad cap, training backlog, `readyTroops`, `threatTroops` (a full scan of `world.squads` for hostile troops within 40 tiles of the base, 283–295), `isolated` (no hostile faction reachable by land/amphibious paths, 296–297), `invasion` from `AiOperations.recruitmentObjective`.
2. `attainable` = resources whose deposits the player owns **and** has researched, mine or not (302–310). This is the "renewable" set for dependency quotes.
3. `militaryPosture` (`AiMilitaryPosture.ts`): `protectedGold` = next age price + the most expensive outstanding node in each tree; `wealthy` = EarlyModern+ with ≥ max(250k, cap×5000) spare gold → unlocks a separate recruit lane and a larger army target.
4. `guardCities` (Modern only) leases up to two anti-air squads per city for 5 s.
5. `militaryDemand` (`AiMilitaryDemand.ts` 23–153): desired units per role from personality weights, invasion objective, posture; equipment per missing recruit (+ a 1–2 kit buffer) and per planned refit; Modern missile-defence steel, payloads, oil. It then runs the bounded dependency quote (`AiProductionDependencies.beginMaterials/stepMaterials`, 93–143) with a 64-unit allowance per decision, resumable across decisions via `demandPlanning` (keyed by a `JSON.stringify` of age/research/buildings/protected items, 313). The quote walks recipe graphs (bronze ← copper+tin, steel ← iron+carbon ← ironOre…) and produces `materials`, `bottlenecks` (`workshop|research|resource|incoming|protected|capacity|unaffordable|limited|cycle`) and `timeToOutput`. So **yes, the AI understands resource dependencies**, but only for deposits it already owns; unowned deposits make a chain "unavailable" and the unit simply drops out of `availableRoles` (AiMilitaryDemand.ts 59–85).
6. `economicCandidates` (`AiEconomicPlanner.ts` 52–349) produces ≤24 intents and returns ≤4 (+ a development + ≤3 wealthy recruits):
   - **Construct** intents from `AiPlacementCandidates.candidates` (≤8 sites; see below), valued by `site.objective × doctrine.economy[type]` plus situational bonuses (city when reserve-starved, factory when ore is stockpiled, barracks/archery/… when kits are waiting and training backlog > 20 s, workshop when inputs are ready). If the building's technology is missing, a **research** intent for it is emitted instead (176–194).
   - **Recruit** intents per role with a deficit: newest researched unit whose kit is in stock, from the producer with the shortest queue (218–279). Benefit 4000/7000/10000 (normal/invasion/threat).
   - **Research**: for each tree in `personality.researchOrder`, every non-rejected technology is scored by `researchUtility` and the best positive one is emitted; if a **progression race** has started (`progressionRaceStarted`: any faction has two trees or a later age), the cheapest two-tree pair is computed (282–290) and race nodes get +8000 and are sorted ahead.
   - **Advance** (317–330) is emitted whenever `advanceRejection` with infinite gold passes, benefit 2500 (+1500 scholar). The final sort (333–341) puts emergency first, then **`advance` unconditionally**, then race nodes, then `production-prerequisite:` constructs, then committed over growth (when the army is safe), then score. `usableNextAge` (`AiResearchUtility.ts` 202–233) is exported but **never called** (grep), so "viable" age-ups are not actually gated on a usable next-age chain.
   - `researchUtility` (`AiResearchUtility.ts` 36–200) sums: unit unlock/modernization reachable through the prerequisite graph (3200 per missing unit, if its kit is attainable and its producer exists/is researched), production-bottleneck recipes (6500), owned-resource access (4500), naval items when a coast is usable (warship 8000 if sea threat, transport 5000 if goods ≥10, logistics 3500, port 4500, fleet AA 8000), economic city/factory/throughput (5000/4500/4500), late-game capability (8000), observed-defense (6000), and a flat `age-prerequisite` 900 + slot×150 for every non-naval technology. Each call constructs one `AiProductionDependencies` plus one more per candidate unit, and recurses `availability` up to 32 nodes.
7. **Spending** (357–478): the wealthy lane buys up to three affordable recruits immediately. Otherwise `military.decide` (refits/modernization) may consume the slot when nothing is "developing". Then the saving goal: the top candidate (or the retained goal) is reserved in the `AiBudgetLedger` with `amounts = min(liquid, cost)`; **if it is not yet affordable, the director returns having issued no command** (449–470) — except that a recruit goal lets one affordable research/advance through (460–468). Goals are abandoned on generation change, expiry (research/advance 600 s, others 120 s), 60 s without funding progress, or when the research is no longer legal/useful; they are displaced only by emergencies, prerequisites, an advance, a race node, committed-over-growth, or (non-development goals only) a 20 % better score (396–411). A research/advance goal therefore **hoards all liquid gold and blocks construction and recruitment until paid** (confirmed by `AiProgressionPolicy.test.ts` 53–82, which asserts `gold` stays reserved and `refitting` is never called).
8. Accepted commands pay through the normal domain handlers; the reservation is released immediately after `applyCommand` (475).

### Placement (`AiPlacementCandidates.candidates`, 92–342)

A per-player type cursor walks 21 building types, testing at most 8 tiles per decision with the authoritative `world.buildingSite` (full `buildRejection` + `constructionRejection`) and `AiRecovery.canBuild` (loss backoff + local threat). Objectives: first-of-type with researched units 6000; first producer of a demanded output 5000; `missingProducer` 12000 (reason `production-prerequisite:`); wealthy support capacity 9000; cities when reserve income is below the planned army's needs; trade growth only with completed-trip evidence (`AiTradeOpportunities.best` + `tradeSupplyLimited`, 211–231); isolated factions get ports/cities; Modern types get fixed objectives. Tiles come from "land pages" (`ownedLandNearest(base, 64, after)`), i.e. **always the 64 owned tiles nearest the capital, paging outward**; ports use an ownership-maintained coast index (`changed` hook from `Simulation.changeOwner`, 4840); mines/oil use owned deposits sorted by `extractionPriority` (stone only after a siege workshop, horses second). Missile-defence stacks around cities. There is no frontier/strategic placement, no spacing beyond the authoritative footprint rules, and no distance-to-deposit consideration for factories.

### Coast acquisition (`AiCoastPlanner`, 150–590)

For a nation with no usable owned coast or no port, one goal per player: **scan every valid port anchor × water edge on the whole map** (`edges` built in the constructor from `coast.connections()` × the port footprint, 61–73), 8 edges per tick for the one player the round-robin picked that tick; each edge is checked with `legal` (footprint ownership/war rules), `fortifications.blocked`, `resourceSites.rejection`, and `paths.connected`/`squadPaths.connected`; edges on seas with a foreign port (via `navalFacts.readSea("buildings")`) or any edge when the player has no port join a shortlist of 8 scored by Manhattan distance to the base. After the full scan, the best legal candidate gets an exact route quote (`AiRouteQuotes`), 2–4 idle squads are leased and marched there, ownership is awaited, then a port and a nearby factory are funded through the ledger. The goal has a hard 300 s deadline (188) and restarts 40 s after finishing or aborting.

### Trade (`AiTradeOpportunities`, `Trade`)

The AI does not choose trade routes. `Trade` (civilian, identical for humans and AI) spawns couriers per site when stock reaches the shipment capacity (`spawn`, 1019–1070), picks markets with its own shortlist/risk logic, and records a `TradeCycleQuote` per actor on completed trips (1346–1348, `observedRisk` is currently always 0 at that call site). `AiTradeOpportunities.step` (69–109) walks ≤8 actors per tick and keeps the best fresh (≤30 s) completed-trip quote per player per mode; `best()` re-validates both buildings' existence/ownership/health each read, so it holds IDs, not references. The director uses it for: `supplyLimitedSources` (producer starvation evidence), factory/port/city growth objectives, naval theater scoring, and the naval investment income estimate. The AI never issues trade controls (no `trade-*` commands in `domain/Ai*`). Piracy: `Trade.step` captures a courier when any hostile combatant is within 1.2 tiles (1226–1256); the naval planner's `raid-trade` objective (641–651) sails a dominant fleet at the most valuable hostile courier within 48 tiles.

### Naval (`AiNavalFacts`, `AiNavalPlanner`, `AiNavalBombardment`)

- `AiNavalFacts` keeps per-sea registries of coastal buildings (any building whose port footprint touches walkable water, `portSea` 198–202), ships and ship jobs, as O(1)-resumable `NavalFactSequence` linked lists, updated by authoritative hooks (`Simulation.ts` 384–398, 2607, 3432–3433) plus a periodic full re-scan (`step`, 250–339; budget 32–48/tick; a pass is ~buildings+ships+jobs+registry sizes units). Refs are stored but `EntityCollection` mutates records in place (`EntityCollection.ts` 35–66), so the refs stay valid; ownership is re-read from `world.owners` on every consumer path I traced.
- `AiNavalPlanner.step(16)` (265–500) picks **one enabled faction per tick** (nations and tribes). A faction with no mission runs `chooseTheater` (127–167): read all owned coastal buildings, then for each of ≤8 seas **every coastal building, every ship and every job in that sea** (all owners), scoring owned value + cargo + trade evidence + enemy power × `navalWeight` − losses. Then a mission (`defend-port`) runs a resumable assessment over the same sequences, and `applyAssessment` (501–704) chooses fund / assemble / stage (patrol groups of ≤8 over `navalAnchors` sectors with `coastalPatrol` routes) / execute (intercept when ready power ≥ 1.3× local enemy) / raid-trade / bombard (when the sea is quiet for 15 s, ≥2 ships, a structure-capable shooter and an offensive target). Every mission has `deadline = created + 120 s` (310) and is aborted at the deadline (317–320), which `stop-ships` all owned ready ships, releases leases, and waits 20 s before a new mission and a fresh theater scan.
- Funding (`fund`, 831–962): only the newest researched **warship** whose age the gathering port supports; `committed < shipCap`; a per-(player,sea) investment credit (initial 4×cost, replenished at 35 % of estimated income, 60 s windows) and a loss pause; a ledger reservation (emergency priority if a threat is near the port) and `recruit-ship` at the least-busy same-sea port.
- **Transports are never purchased by the economic AI**: the only `recruit-ship` emitters are `AiNavalPlanner.fund` (warships), the non-economy fallback `Expansion.thinkCapabilities` (warships only, 1691–1728) and the dead `Skirmish.developAi`. Overseas movement relies on amphibious squads (`Simulation.amphibious` = a researched shore transport definition, 432–436; `AiArmyPlanner` 136).
- Ships leased to a mission are moved only by the mission; `Simulation.moveShip` (2680–2700) uses `fleetLane` for formation lanes and `allowsLocalPursuit` gates autonomous pursuit (3342–3343).

### Checkpointing

`AiEconomicDirector.checkpoint/restore` (107–180) covers routes, trade evidence, coast goals, ledger, leases, losses, recovery, cities, defenses, fronts, modern fronts, naval facts (without refs; re-bound from the live world), naval planner (lanes rebuilt), military/army planner, demands, demand planning (including the resumable dependency quote), savings, placement cursors and land pages (coast index rebuilt from `world.owners`), decision cursor and `nextDecision`. `AiCoastPlanner.edges` and `AiPlacementCandidates.coast` are recomputed from the map. Checkpoints are used every commit by `HostedRuntime` (`HostedRuntime.ts` 102–127) and by the commit verifier, so this completeness matters for the hosted path even though there is no restart recovery.

## Strengths

- The ledger/lease split is a genuinely good design: quotes never mint credit, every purchase goes through the authoritative command handlers, and reservations/leases are scoped by `(playerId, generation)` so AI↔human handover (`setAiController`, Simulation.ts 225–240) cannot leave a controller acting on stale authority. `AiEconomy.test.ts` 192–226 and 347–358 pin this down.
- Stale-reference discipline is strong in this area: `AiTradeOpportunities.best`, `AiNavalPlanner` (`m.port`, members, targets), `AiCoastPlanner` (`legal`, `owned`, member re-validation), `AiRecovery` and `AiCityRecords.valid` all re-read ownership/existence from the world on every use. I found **no** instance of the match-269 bug class (cached mutable building reference dereferenced after an ownership change to 0) in the files in scope.
- The dependency quote (`AiProductionDependencies`) is the right abstraction: bounded, resumable, with explicit bottleneck reasons that feed both placement (`production-prerequisite`) and research (`production-bottleneck`), and tests cover the bronze/iron/steel chains (`AiEconomy.test.ts` 114–160, 227–304).
- Placement is cheap per decision (≤8 authoritative site checks) and uses ownership-maintained indexes rather than map scans.
- `AiNavalFacts` is a careful piece of engineering (O(1) hooks, resumable cursors that survive removals, restore preserves cursor order) and is well tested.
- Automatic production is deterministic, insertion-order independent and shared with humans; AI demand only raises targets and protected inputs only lower the spendable budget.

## Issues

Severity: critical / high / medium / low. Confidence: confirmed (traced end to end or measured) / suspected.

### I1. Naval planning work is a fixed 16 units per tick for the whole match, so fleet assessment cadence collapses with faction count and sea size — `AiEconomicDirector.ts:212–215`, `AiNavalPlanner.ts:265–279, 127–167, 355–360` — **high, confirmed (mechanism) / measured (scale)**

`naval.step(16)` serves one faction per call and each call charges one unit per fact read. A theater choice reads every coastal building, ship and ship job in up to eight seas; an assessment re-reads all owned coastal buildings plus every ship and job in the mission sea. On the Mediterranean 500×250 with 52 factions those sequences are hundreds of entries (see benchmark), so one assessment needs tens of turns and each faction gets a turn every ~44 ticks. Measured: with 45 factions in Bronze/Classical (largest sea: 32 coastal buildings, 21 ships) an assessment already takes 40–44 ticks (5 s) mean and 83 max, i.e. 8–16× the 5 s re-assessment interval is spent waiting on budget rather than deciding. At the 8 Hz reference load (≈300 warships and ≈400 coastal buildings concentrated in one sea) the same arithmetic gives ≈500 reads per assessment ≈ 30 turns × 45 ticks ≈ 1 350 ticks ≈ 170 s per assessment per faction. Consequence: fleets react to threats and resume patrols minutes late; the `nextAssessment = +5 s` intent (494) is unreachable; and because every mission dies at its 120 s deadline (I2), a large share of fleet time is spent idle in `abort`/theater-scan.

### I2. Every fleet mission is aborted at a hard 120 s deadline, which stops all its ships and forces a 20 s idle plus a full theater rescan — `AiNavalPlanner.ts:310, 317–320, 237–263, 286–296` — **high, confirmed**

`deadline: tick + secondsToTicks(120)` is never extended; at the deadline `transition(m, "abort", "mission deadline expired")` issues `stop-ships` for every owned ready ship (252–257), releases leases, and sets `nextAssessment = +20 s`; the next `step` releases the mission and starts `chooseTheater` from scratch. The patrol/intercept state machine is therefore periodically reset regardless of what the fleet was doing (a patrol group half-way along its route, an interception in progress, a bombardment). `tests/skirmish/AiNavalPlanner.test.ts` 166–178 only advances 50 s, so the test name "beyond the old port-defense timeout" does not cover the 120 s abort. Combined with I1, at 52 factions a fleet spends most of its life waiting.

### I3. Coast acquisition scans every port-capable coast edge on the map at 8 per tick for one faction per tick, cannot finish before its own 300 s deadline on large maps, and restarts forever — `AiCoastPlanner.ts:61–73, 150–158, 188, 197–244` — **high, confirmed (arithmetic) / measured (edge count)**

`edges` holds every (port anchor, water tile) pair on the map (12 203 on Mediterranean 500×250, 29 831 on Migration 1000×1000, measured). `step(8)` serves one player per call, and `AiEconomicDirector.step` calls it once per tick, so a given player's goal advances 8 edges every `players.length` ticks. With 20 factions that is ~0.4 edges/tick → ~960 edges inside the 300 s deadline; with 52 factions ~370. The goal then aborts with "Coastal acquisition deadline reached", sleeps 40 s and restarts at `cursor: 0`. Net effect: **no landlocked or port-less nation ever acquires a coast through this planner on a large map**, while the planner still burns its allowance every tick. Each edge test also calls `legal` (a footprint loop), `fortifications.blocked`, `resourceSites.rejection` and up to two connectivity checks. There is no test for `AiCoastPlanner` (grep of `tests/skirmish` finds none).

### I4. One economic decision per two ticks for the entire match, one command per decision — `AiEconomicDirector.ts:227–239` — **high, confirmed**

`secondsToTicks(.15)` is 2 at 8 Hz, and the loop `break`s after the first `decide`. So the match-wide ceiling is 4 decisions/s, each normally issuing at most one command (the wealthy lane is EarlyModern+ with ≥250 k spare gold). With 14 nations each wanting a decision every 3 s (4.67/s needed) the cadence already degrades; with promoted tribes it degrades linearly (44 nations → one decision every ~11 s each). A human issues an opening of city + barracks + mine + research ×3 + recruits in ten seconds; an AI nation needs 30–90 s for the same, and in mid-game every rebuild, refit, recruit and research competes for one slot. Measured: 2 400 decisions in 4 800 ticks with 15→27 regular factions (slot saturated throughout) and only 24 % of decisions produced a command; 108 construction commands in ten minutes for ~20 nations.

### I5. Saving for research or an age-up reserves all liquid gold and suspends construction, recruitment and refits for up to ten minutes — `AiEconomicDirector.ts:396–470`, `AiEconomicPlanner.ts:97, 333–341` — **high (design), confirmed**

A research/advance goal (`committed`, 600 s expiry) is displaced only by emergency recruits, `production-prerequisite` constructs, another advance or race node; `amounts = min(liquid, cost)` reserves everything the player has; when unaffordable `decide` returns without a command (469). The ledger reservation also removes that gold from the army planner (`AiArmyPlanner.ts:542`) and `AiCoastPlanner`/`AiNavalPlanner.fund` spendable checks. With `goldIncome = 20 + land/40` per second (Simulation.ts 3684–3688), a 40 000 gold Late-Medieval advance at 100 gold/s is ~7 minutes of total economic paralysis apart from automatic production. `AiProgressionPolicy.test.ts` 53–82 encodes this as intended, and the hotfix note explains why (trade revenue drop), but at the 52-faction target it means most nations are frozen most of the time in the mid game. Measured: at the 10-minute sample 19 of 27 regular factions were holding a saving goal; research episodes averaged 31 s (max 146 s) and recruit episodes 23 s (max 200 s, the 120 s expiry followed by an immediate re-reservation of the same intent in the same `decide`). Recruit goals are the common case because a recruit costs 12 reserve units (`scaledRecipeCost`, Manpower.ts 197–211) while reserve income is `(18 + 12 × cities) × 12/1000` per second in Bronze (`applyReservePoints`, Simulation.ts 2471–2476), i.e. one squad per 20–55 s, and while that goal waits only research/advance may pass (460–468) — construction cannot.

### I6. `advance` is sorted ahead of every non-emergency candidate unconditionally and `usableNextAge` is dead code — `AiEconomicPlanner.ts:335`, `AiResearchUtility.ts:202–233` — **medium, confirmed**

The sort key `Number(b.kind === "advance") − Number(a.kind === "advance")` ignores `score`, so as soon as two trees are complete the AI commits its whole treasury to the age-up (I5), even when it has no producer for the next age's units and no deposits for their kits. `usableNextAge` was written for exactly this gate but nothing calls it. Consequence: age-ups that unlock nothing usable while the army stays on old equipment; a scholar/balanced nation can be in Classical with Bronze-only kits for minutes.

### I7. Territory expansion and war targeting are not resource-directed, so a nation without copper+tin (or iron, or carbon) never gets the kit chain and silently fields older units — `HomeTerritory.ts:105–135`, `AiMilitaryDemand.ts:59–85`, `AiEconomicDirector.ts:302–310` — **medium (gameplay), confirmed**

`attainable` only includes deposits the player already owns; `HomeTerritory.goal` scores frontier tiles purely by distance to base/current position; `AiOperations` targets are not deposit-aware (grep). When bronze is unavailable the bronze units drop out of `availableRoles`, `researchUtility` gives their technologies no unlock value, and the nation keeps Stone Age infantry with no plan to fix it. Humans route around this by claiming or capturing deposits.

### I8. Elimination never releases a faction's AI economic state — `Simulation.ts:5431–5445` (checkWinner), `AiEconomicDirector.ts:181–195, 228–239, 259–263` — **low, confirmed**

`economy.release` is called only from `setAiController` (Simulation.ts 237). `step` skips non-enabled players (`continue`, 231–235) and `decide` is never reached, so the `release` inside `decide` (260–263) does not fire on elimination. Lingering state: `demands`, `demandPlanning`, `saving`, `nextDecision`, `naval.missions` (step only picks enabled players, so an eliminated player's mission is never aborted), `coasts.goals`, `tradeQuotes.evidence`, `losses`. Ledger reservations and leases do expire by tick/existence, so no authority leaks; the cost is checkpoint size and a permanently-held `missions` entry per dead faction. (Note the pop-up-faction plan will add and eliminate factions regularly.)

### I9. Hard-coded tick thresholds in the recovery state did not follow the 8 Hz change — `AiNavalPlanner.ts:569–575` — **low, confirmed**

`age>=400` / `age>=800` / `age<200` are raw tick counts (50 s / 100 s / 25 s at 8 Hz, 40/80/20 s at 10 Hz) while every other timer in the file uses `secondsToTicks`. `AiLossWindow.ts:29` has a stale "50-tick bins" comment (bins are `secondsToTicks(2.5)` = 20 ticks).

### I10. A mission that is merely waiting for naval credit is driven to `abort` after 100 s, compounding I2 — `AiNavalPlanner.ts:883–889, 574` — **medium, suspected**

`fund` transitions to `recover` when `investmentCredit < costGold` or during a loss pause; the `recover` branch of `applyAssessment` aborts when `age>=800 && !enough`. For a poor faction that is the normal path, so the mission churns: create → theater scan → assess → fund → recover → abort (stop-ships) → 20 s → repeat. Each cycle is a full theater scan under I1.

### I11. Raid-trade and interception declare war through the alliance command when `canTarget` fails — `AiNavalPlanner.ts:646–647, 662–663, 684–685` — **low, suspected**

Under `raids-v1` `canTarget` is `diplomacy.hostile`, and `raidTargets` already filters by `hostile`, so the declaration is unreachable for raids. Under legacy diplomacy (`hostile` = non-allied) a quiet navy would formally declare war on any non-allied faction whose loaded courier passes within 48 tiles. Worth a guard if legacy rules remain selectable.

### I12. `candidates` recomputes trade evidence and market status for every building type tested — `AiPlacementCandidates.ts:209–223` — **low (perf), confirmed**

`factories`, `ports`, `productiveLand`, `productiveSea` and `growth()` (which calls `trade.sourceStatus` → `Trade.refreshMarkets`, 369–420, plus `site.buildings.reduce`) are evaluated inside the per-type loop although they do not depend on `type`. Up to 2×8 `refreshMarkets` calls per decision; cheap when markets are unchanged but it is wasted work on the hottest AI path. Similarly the port branch rebuilds and sorts the full `anchors` set (283–291) every time the port type is tested.

### I13. `AiRouteQuotes` restored with `requested: true` cannot complete until expiry — `AiRouteQuotes.ts:144–148, 222–237` — **low, suspected**

`restore` keeps `requested: true` quotes but the `domainRoutes` queue is not part of the director's checkpoint (and `Simulation.domainRoutes` is created fresh per instance), so a restored quote waits for a completion that never arrives until `expires` (60 s), after which it is re-requested. Only matters for the hosted-runtime checkpoint path and tests.

### I14. Background budgets are absolute constants that do not scale with the faction count or the tick budget — `AiEconomicDirector.ts:206–226` — **medium (design/perf), confirmed**

The 112/128-unit allowance was tuned for small matches. Measured: the entire `economy.step` including decisions costs 1.1 ms of a 37 ms tick with 45 factions (0.8 ms with 20). In other words the director leaves >95 % of the tick unused while its planners starve (I1, I3). There is no measurement-based (ms) budget and no per-faction fairness other than round-robin.

### I15. Research for tribes is catalogue-order, not utility — `Expansion.ts:1545–1547` — **low (gameplay), confirmed**

`technologies.find(t => t.age === state.age && !researchRejection(...))` researches whatever comes first in `TECHNOLOGIES`, so every tribe follows the same path and can spend gold on e.g. naval nodes while landlocked. When tribes are promoted the director inherits that research.

### I16. Nations cap themselves at two cities before EarlyModern, so manpower (reserves) throttles every army and gold has no sink — `AiPlacementCandidates.ts:198, 201–202, 228–232, 294`; `AiEconomicPlanner.ts:116–138` — **high (gameplay), confirmed**

For `city`, `objective` is 5000 only when `!count`; otherwise it stays 0 unless the faction is `wealthy` (EarlyModern+ with ≥250 k spare gold), `isolated` (then `count < 3`), or trade evidence shows returned cargo above city receiving capacity. With `objective === 0 && count >= 2` the type is skipped outright (294). A second city is emitted with objective 0 and only wins when `reserveShortage > 0` adds its bonus. Reserve income is `(base + 12 × cities) × 12/1000` per second, so with two cities a Bronze nation makes one 12-reserve squad every ~24 s regardless of treasury, and every decision in between is a recruit saving goal (I5). This is why the benchmark shows 100–250 k gold hoards, 19/27 factions in saving, and ~5 buildings per nation in ten minutes. A human builds cities continuously for manpower and territory. (The non-economy fallback uses `economicBuildingTarget(personality, type, squadCount)` at Expansion.ts 1386 — `AiPersonality.ts` 66–74, `economicCopies` cities per personality — but the director never consults it.)

### I17. The "wealthy" spending lane is age-gated to EarlyModern — `AiMilitaryPosture.ts:31–34` — **medium (design), confirmed**

`wealthy` requires `age >= EarlyModern` and ≥ max(250 k, cap×5000) spare gold. A Classical nation sitting on 247 k (benchmark) therefore never gets the multi-recruit lane, the support-capacity placement (`AiPlacementCandidates.ts:200`), the city-for-manpower rule (201–202) or the larger army target. Making the threshold relative to the age price (e.g. ≥ 3 × next `ADVANCES` gold) would let early-age treasuries convert into power.

### I18. Tribes attempt ~43 build commands per second match-wide — `Expansion.ts:1553–1566` — **low (perf), measured**

`thinkTribeDevelopment` tries up to 16 candidate tiles per type per pass with `applyCommand` (each running `buildRejection` + `constructionRejection`), and the benchmark counted 25 830 tribe `build` attempts in ten minutes versus 1 095 recruits. Using `world.buildingSite` as a cheap pre-check (as the director does) or remembering the last rejected tile per type would remove most of it. Out of the director's scope but it shares the AI tick budget.

## Recommendations

Ranked by player-visible effect per engineering day (my estimate in parentheses). "Day" = one engineer-day including tests.

1. **Scale the background allowance with enabled factions and make naval/coast budgets per faction** (0.5–1 day). Replace the constant 16/8/48 with `perFaction × enabledCount` (e.g. naval 16 per enabled faction, capped by a measured ms budget: stop when `performance.now()`-style tick time, already available through `diagnosticPhase`, exceeds ~3 ms). Also let `naval.step` and `coasts.step` serve more than one faction per tick when budget remains. Fixes I1/I3/I14 scale collapse; the benchmark shows the economy director currently costs well under a millisecond per tick.
2. **Let nations build cities for manpower and territory** (0.5–1 day). In `AiPlacementCandidates.candidates` give `city` a positive objective whenever `snapshot.reserveIncome` (squads/s) is below `demand`-implied need (e.g. `missing × 12 / 120 s`) or `count < economicBuildingTarget(personality, "city", squadCount)` (`AiPersonality.ts` 66–74), remove the `count >= 2` skip for cities, and drop the EarlyModern gate on `wealthy` (I17). Fixes I16; expect the largest single improvement in army size and gold utilisation (benchmark: 100–250 k idle gold at 10 min).
3. **Make fleet missions persistent** (0.5 day). Extend `deadline` on every successful assessment (e.g. `deadline = tick + 120 s` inside `applyAssessment`), keep it as a watchdog only, and skip `stop-ships` when a mission is replaced by a new one in the same sea. Fixes I2/I10. Add a test that advances 5 minutes of quiet patrol and asserts no `stop-ships` and no theater rescan.
4. **Replace the global coast-edge scan with an owner-local candidate generator** (1 day). Start from the player's territory boundary (`AiBoundaryIndex` chunks already exist) and the nearest coast tiles of `world.coast.waterEdges` per reachable sea component, bounded to e.g. 256 edges per goal; score by distance; fall back to foreign-port seas only when `navalFacts.readSea` says one exists. Add the missing `AiCoastPlanner` tests. Fixes I3.
5. **Decouple the decision slot from the command slot** (1–2 days). Keep one `decide` per nation per 3 s but let a decision issue up to N commands from disjoint budgets (one construct, one research, up to k recruits) and run decisions for `ceil(enabled/12)` players per slot so each nation keeps its 3 s cadence at 44 factions. Fixes I4. Guard with the existing "one faction per global slot" test rewritten to the new contract.
6. **Reserve a saving rate, not the whole balance** (1 day). When the goal is research/advance, reserve `min(cost − funded, income × 60 s)` per decision rather than all liquid gold, and let growth candidates spend the unreserved remainder; keep the full reservation only when `funded ≥ 0.8 × cost`. Preserves the hotfix's intent (no infrastructure spam while a viable age-up is close) without the multi-minute freeze (I5). Measure with `pacingAutoplay` seeds 47/72 before and after; the hotfix doc gives the baseline numbers.
7. **Gate `advance` on `usableNextAge` (or score it)** (0.5 day). Call the existing function; when false, drop the unconditional sort precedence so research that makes the next age usable wins first. Fixes I6.
8. **Resource-directed expansion** (2–3 days). In `HomeTerritory.goal` add a bonus for frontier tiles within r of an unowned deposit whose resource is in `demand.bottlenecks` with reason `resource`; expose that list from the director (`production(playerId)` already returns demand). For nations boxed in, let `AiOperations` weight rivals that own a needed deposit. Fixes I7 and is the single biggest competence gap versus a human in the pre-modern game.
9. **Release AI state on elimination** (0.25 day). Call `expansion.economy.release(player.id)` and `operations.release` in `checkWinner` for each newly defeated player. Fixes I8; required before pop-up factions.
10. **Tick-rate hygiene** (0.25 day). Convert the raw tick constants in `AiNavalPlanner.ts:569–575` to `secondsToTicks`, fix the `AiLossWindow` comment, and add a lint-style test that greps `domain/Ai*.ts` for `>=\s*\d{3,}` comparisons against `world.tick` differences. Fixes I9.
11. **Hoist per-decision invariants out of the type loop in `candidates`** (0.25 day) and reuse `snapshot.buildings` counts via a `Map<BuildingType, number>` built once. Fixes I12; small but on the hottest AI path.

Lower priority: a `trade-control` policy for AI (pause sea trade when `observedRisk`/losses exceed a threshold, since the AI currently keeps feeding pirates); make tribe research utility-based by reusing `researchUtility` with an empty demand; consider a transport purchase path if amphibious squads are ever tech-gated late.

## Competence versus a human (summary)

Where the AI is competent: it never cheats (every purchase is authoritative and quoted), it understands the full kit chain for deposits it owns, it builds the missing workshop/mine/factory when a demanded output is absent, it researches toward units it can actually equip and produce, it keeps an emergency-recruit reflex, its fleets patrol sensibly in small matches and buy warships within an income-bounded allowance, and it reuses the same automatic production allocator as humans. Checkpoint coverage of its state is complete apart from the restored-quote stall (I13).

Where a competent human beats it, in order of impact: (1) macro throughput — one command every 3–7 s versus dozens in the opening, and long "saving" freezes; (2) manpower and territory — a human builds cities continuously, the AI stops at two until EarlyModern (I16) and therefore fields roughly a quarter of the army its gold could pay for; (3) resources — a human claims or captures copper/tin/iron/carbon, the AI only ever uses what it happens to own (I7); (4) timing of age-ups — a human ages up when the next tier is usable, the AI as soon as it is legal (I6); (5) naval persistence and reaction time at scale (I1–I3); (6) trade — the AI neither protects nor steers trade beyond building capacity. The ranked recommendations above are the top ten improvements by player-visible effect per engineering day; items 1–4 are each under a day and together address the scale collapse and the manpower ceiling that dominate what a player sees.

## Open questions

- Is the 120 s mission deadline intentional as a liveness watchdog (e.g. against a stuck `execute`), or a leftover from the earlier "port-defense timeout"? The test name suggests the latter.
- Was the one-decision-per-slot rule (`AiEconomy.test.ts` 347) meant as a determinism/perf guard or as a pacing device? If pacing, the saving freeze (I5) is doing most of that work already.
- Does the owner want tribes to run the full director after promotion with their catalogue-order research history, or should promotion trigger a research re-plan?
- Is `observedRisk` ever non-zero? `Trade.ts:1348` writes 0; if risk is intended to feed `riskAdjustedGoldPer1000Ticks` for naval investment, the AI currently sees all sea trade as risk-free.
- For the 1000×1000 Migration map the coast-edge count and sea sequences will be ~4× the Mediterranean numbers; recommendation 1/3 should be validated there.
