# Late Medieval regional references

The user approved Early Medieval and requested Late Medieval on October 2, 2026.
The set uses fourteenth-to-fifteenth-century Novgorod/Pskov material cues while
preserving existing building roles and the strict overhead art direction.

## Research and visual use

- [Fourteenth-century Novgorod estate reconstruction, M. I. Petrov, Institute of Archaeology RAS](https://archaeolog.ru/media/periodicals/agis/AGIS-11/Petrov/petrov.html):
  describes the Duboshin-II estate, including a log building dated 1363/64,
  brick heating remains and timber floor/courtyard surfaces. Use more developed
  timber household groups, attached entry/storage rooflets, wooden circulation
  and restrained brick hearth/chimney accents. The study explicitly treats
  upper stories, entrances and other elevation details as hypothetical. Its
  reconstruction is a regional design reference, not an exact blueprint for
  these icons. Unlike the Early Medieval set, this age fits the study's period.
- [Novgorod Museum announcement of the estate reconstruction](https://novgorodmuseum.ru/o-muzee-zapovednike/novosti/na-arheologicheskoj-konferencii-v-moskve-byla-predstavlena-trekhmernaya-rekonstrukciya-novgorodskoj-usadby):
  identifies the reconstruction as fourteenth-century and its excavation as
  work conducted in 2017-2019. This checks the period and archaeological context
  rather than providing proof of any exact roof treatment.
- [Pskov Kremlin, Pskov Museum](https://www.museumpskov.ru/locations?slug=ansambl_pskovskogo_kremlya):
  the indexed museum page dates portions of the Kremlin walls and towers to
  the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries and cites stone-wall construction in
  1393. Use coarse limestone enclosure tops and a stronger round defensive
  tower silhouette. Direct page retrieval timed out, so only the indexed
  museum extract was used. Later additions and modern restoration roofs are
  not treated as evidence for the original medieval appearance.

## Game interpretation

Dark smoked-brown wooden shingles, burgundy accents, repeated iron straps,
household combinations, specialized recruitment compounds and corner platforms
create a coherent visual age and stronger role cues. Exact roof covering and
trim are design choices rather than an attested reconstruction. The town keeps
its gathering square and household silhouette without letting a church or
large palace dominate the population-building cue.

The single broader cargo vessel, small aft deckhouse, three factory chimneys,
kiln layout, extraction capstan, armor arrangements and crossbow placement
continue gameplay roles; their exact forms are not attributed to excavated
Novgorod or Pskov plans. The counterweight trebuchet, covered mobile tower and
ram follow the game's siege progression. This research did not establish a
specific late Rus machine design.

Military boundaries are drawn as flat horizontal cap strips. The defensive
tower is an exposed round fighting deck with a small canopy, an intentional
readability treatment rather than a literal roof reconstruction. Later
Muscovite onion-domed silhouettes, seventeenth-century decorative timberwork,
modern emblems, flags and firearms are excluded from the set.
