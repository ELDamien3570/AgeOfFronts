# Early Modern Russian building references

The user moved on from Late Medieval to Early Modern on October 2, 2026.
This set uses sixteenth–seventeenth-century Muscovite and northern trade-court
references, adapted to existing game roles and the approved overhead camera.

- [Moscow Kremlin architecture, Kremlin Museums](https://vision.kreml.ru/research/world-heritage/moskovskiy-kreml-i-krasnaya-ploshchad-35-let-v-yunesko/): the museum dates the red-brick wall and tower construction to 1485–1495, with tented stone tops added in the seventeenth century. Brick construction therefore predates this age and is an inherited regional cue, not a claim that it was invented in Early Modern. Use brick/cream masonry and stronger roof/platform footprints. Direct retrieval timed out; the indexed museum article was used. Its later eighteenth/nineteenth-century buildings are excluded.
- [Seventeenth-century infantry berdysh, State Historical Museum, object 4915 or](https://catalog.shm.ru/entity/OBJECT/1667770): the Russian object is dated to the seventeenth century and made from iron and wood. Broad curved poleaxe shapes add a recognizable equipment cue to the infantry barracks and Armory. This is an object reference, not evidence for a barracks layout.
- [Craft and trade in the sixteenth–seventeenth centuries, State Historical Museum](https://nav.shm.ru/halls/143/): the indexed exhibition description identifies specialized trades and larger state manufactures including cannon, printing, armory and mint establishments. Use organized work courts, textile manufacture and differentiated artillery/weapon production. Direct retrieval was unavailable, so this use is limited to the indexed museum description.
- [Arkhangelsk merchant courts, Arkhangelsk Regional Museum](https://kraeved29.ru/objekty/): the merchant court/fortress complex was built in 1668–1683 and combined trade, administrative and defensive functions. Larger brick warehouse courts and a substantial cargo quay inform the Port; these icons do not reproduce the nine-hectare complex or its present rebuilt appearance.
- [Russian artillery collection, Military Historical Museum of Artillery](https://www.old.artillery-museum.ru/ru/museum/history/): the collection includes early Russian iron firearms, dated late-fifteenth-century bronze guns and sixteenth–seventeenth-century artillery. Field guns and a shorter howitzer are game-role interpretations; no exact excavated foundry or carriage plan is claimed.

Muted grey-green seam-pattern roof tops, cream cap accents, burgundy cloth,
compound proportions, roof material treatment, exposed inventories and planar
targets are readability choices. They are not a reconstruction of a specific
surviving building. A large church, palace or national emblem would compete
with the intended role cue, so these do not dominate the icons.

The current game content uses firearms for infantry and ranged units in this
age, field cannon/howitzer artillery, and Armory as the troop-equipment
producer. The artwork follows those roles without changing game rules. The
stable retains horses and adds cavalry equipment. Only City, Barracks,
Archery Range and Stables retain recruitment/town enclosures; open production
yards are not given unrelated defensive walls.

All roofs, brick caps, gun carriages, chimneys, piers and upright supports are
projected directly overhead. All original generation masters and subsequent
camera corrections are retained in each building's SourceArt folder.
