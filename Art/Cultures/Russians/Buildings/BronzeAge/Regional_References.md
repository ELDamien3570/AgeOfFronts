# Regional references for Russian Bronze Age game art

Research date: October 2, 2026. These communities occupied parts of the territory
of present-day Russia long before Russian identity. Their material culture
provides regional inspiration, not proof that the game's culture existed then.
The art combines selected cues from different places and periods; it does not
depict one exact archaeological community.

## Craft production and the new blacksmith

Durakov and Mylnikova's study of Stary Tartas-5 in the Baraba forest-steppe
describes household-associated casting areas, smelting hearths/kilns, crucibles,
nozzles and molds. The blacksmith uses exposed casting equipment and a small
clay hearth to distinguish bronze equipment production from general trade goods.
The game's dedicated building is an art/readability choice; the paper does not
establish that these were separate military blacksmith buildings.

[Foundries at Stary Tartas-5](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/20-48-4/annot-08/)
— DOI 10.17746/1563-0110.2020.48.4.084-094.

Molodin and colleagues describe reusable clay bronze-casting molds and casting
areas between houses or within special structures in the Middle Irtysh region.
This supports the mold-and-casting-yard cue; precise mold ornament is not copied.

[Seima-Turbino casting traditions](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/18-46-3/annot-05/)
— DOI 10.17746/1563-0110.2018.46.3.049-058.

## Settlement blocks and wells

Epimakhov, Panteleeva and Koryakova document water wells and densely spaced,
linearly arranged structures at Kamennyi Ambar during the Sintashta-Petrovka
period. The city uses organized household blocks around a visible well.
The exact five-roof arrangement is designed for the game's icon scale.

[Wells at Kamennyi Ambar](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/20-48-4/annot-09/)
— DOI 10.17746/1563-0110.2020.48.4.095-105.

## Mining

Ankusheva and colleagues describe extraction pits, waste dumps, a copper-smelting
furnace and later shaft mining at Novotemirsky in the southern Trans-Urals.
The mine adds a modest shaft opening, ore baskets and spoil piles to the approved
overhead quarry silhouette. Furnace/casting equipment remains the blacksmith's
strong visual cue, even though the archaeological site also had metallurgy.

[Novotemirsky mine](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/21-49-1/annot-04/)
— DOI 10.17746/1563-0110.2021.49.1.030-038.

## Pottery and boundaries of the interpretation

Volkova's analysis documents varied Fatyanovo and mixed local ceramic traditions
in the eastern European forest zone. Pottery is a modest craft cue; the game's
simple impressed marks are artistic interpretation, not a reproduced pattern.

[Bronze Age forest-zone ceramics](https://archaeology.nsc.ru/en/publications/jr-aeae-en/18-46-2/annot-06/)
— DOI 10.17746/1563-0110.2018.46.2.052-059.

Roof shapes, infantry recruitment yards, archery lanes, horse paddocks, ports,
siege workshops and lookout towers retain their gameplay role silhouettes.
The wheeled battering ram and siege tower are game designs, not attested regional
Bronze Age siege-engine reconstructions. The selected palisade enclosures, roof
flues and enlarged cargo boat are user-directed age-progression choices, rather
than forms established by these papers. This set deliberately avoids later Russian domes,
Orthodox symbols, national flags and medieval forge imagery.
